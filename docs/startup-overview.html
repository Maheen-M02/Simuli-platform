1The Idea in One Sentence

We let a software company test their product on realistic AI customers — before a single real customer ever sees it.

A company gives us four things: the link to their product, a test login, a description of who their customer is, and something that customer should be able to do. We put an AI "customer" into a real web browser and let it try. Then we tell them exactly what that customer experienced — where they got confused, where they got stuck, and what to fix.

2The Problem We Are Solving

Most software companies lose the majority of new sign-ups within the first ten minutes. The person opens the product, doesn't understand what to do, and leaves. They never write in to complain — they just disappear.

The company knows that it happened. It almost never knows why.

8.9%Average free-trial-to-paid conversion when no credit card is required[11]
54%Median share of trial users who ever reach the product's "aha" moment[11]
44.7%Drop in sign-up abandonment from one fix: cutting a form from 7 fields to 3[11]

That last number is the whole business in miniature. A single, small, findable problem was costing nearly half the funnel. Someone eventually found it. The question is how much revenue leaked out before they did.

There is a gap in the middle, and it is a big one: nobody tests whether a specific kind of real person can actually figure the product out — cheaply, repeatedly, and before launch. That gap is our product.

3What We Are Building

The company gives us
  • A link to their product
  • A test login
  • Who their customer is
  • What that customer should achieve
We give them back
  • Did the customer succeed?
  • Where they struggled, and why
  • A recording of every step
  • A ranked list of what to fix

In between, our AI takes on a persona — a specific type of customer — opens the product in a real browser, and tries to get the job done the way that person would. It is not following a script. It looks at the screen, decides what a person like that would do next, does it, sees what happened, and continues.

4A Concrete Example

The persona "An Indian small-business owner. Not technical. Has never used accounting software before. Busy, impatient, will give up if confused."
The goal "Create an account, finish the setup, and send your first invoice."
  1. Lands on the homepage, looks for how to sign up, clicks "Start free trial".
  2. Fills in the sign-up form. Hits a password rule it wasn't told about upfront. Retries.
  3. Reaches a setup wizard asking for "GSTIN" and "default ledger account". Doesn't know what a ledger account is. Looks for help. Finds none. Guesses.
  4. Reaches the dashboard. Looks for "create invoice". The button is called "New Document". Takes four clicks to find it.
  5. Fills the invoice. Clicks Save. The page clears with no confirmation — it isn't clear whether it worked.
  6. Gives up, unsure if the invoice was created.

Every one of those moments is a real reason a real customer leaves. The company gets all six, with a screenshot of each, within minutes of asking.

5Why This Is Not Just "Automated Testing"

This distinction is the whole business. It is worth being precise about it.

Traditional QA / test automationWhat we do
Asks: "Does this workflow still work?"Asks: "Can a real customer succeed here — and what do they feel on the way?"
Someone writes the exact steps in advance.Nobody writes the steps. The AI works them out, like a person would.
Passes if the buttons respond.Fails if a human would have been confused, even when everything technically works.
Breaks the moment the UI changes.Adapts — it is looking at the screen, not at a saved list of instructions.
Output: a green tick or a red cross.Output: a customer-experience report with evidence.
Bought by engineering.Bought by product, growth and founders.
The key insightA product can pass every QA test ever written and still lose 70% of its sign-ups. QA tests the software. We test the experience. Those are different products, with different buyers and different budgets.

The part that makes this trustworthy Every finding is labelled with a cause. The system must always classify each problem as one of three things:
  • PRODUCT PROBLEM — the product genuinely confused the customer. This is what we sell.
  • AI LIMITATION — our AI was the one that misunderstood; a real person would not have.
  • TECHNICAL FAILURE — our browser or tooling broke. Not the customer's fault, and not the product's.
If we blur these three together, our reports become noise and nobody trusts them. Keeping them separate — and honestly reporting our own failures — is what makes this a credible business rather than a demo. As far as we can tell from public material, no competitor does this.

Why now, and not two years ago

  • The technology only just became possible. AI agents that can reliably look at a screen, decide, and click became usable in the last 18 months. Before that, this product could not be built.
  • The money has arrived. Over $1.5B of venture capital has gone into AI testing platforms, with 40+ startups now competing[5]. That validates the space — and warns us the window is not wide open forever.

  • Teams got smaller, products didn't. Fewer dedicated researchers and QA staff, same expectation to ship weekly. Something has to fill the gap.
The honest counterweightA crowded, well-funded market means we are not early. We are entering a race already underway. Our advantage cannot be "we thought of it" — it has to be that we do a specific thing better. Section 10 is the most important page in this document.

10Where We Beat Them

Nine specific things. The first three are the ones that actually matter.

1. We tell customers when our own AI was wrong — nobody else does

Every finding is labelled product problem, AI limitation, or technical failure. Every competitor in category B and C has an incentive to hide the second one, because it makes their product look weaker. We publish it. It costs us a little credibility per report and buys us all the credibility that matters: when we say something is a real problem, it can be believed. This is the single hardest thing for a competitor to copy, because copying it means admitting they were hiding it.

2. We make the AI state what it expects before it acts

"I expect this to save my invoice and show a confirmation." Then we compare that to what actually happened. Every mismatch is a candidate usability problem, grounded in something observable rather than in an AI's opinion after the fact. Competitors generate findings by asking a model to summarise a session; we generate them from a recorded contradiction.

3. Every finding must cite the exact step it came from, and we verify the citation exists

If the report references step 12, there is a step 12, with a screenshot. Findings with invalid references are rejected automatically before the customer ever sees them. This kills the biggest failure mode of every AI research tool: fluent, confident, invented insight.

Also betterWhy it wins
4. Real product, real browser, real stateCategory B competitors ask an LLM to imagine your product. We open it. Real error messages, real loading states, real dead ends.
5. Persona-first, not test-case-firstThe QA players start from "here is the flow to check". We start from "here is the person". That produces different findings and, crucially, reaches a different budget.
6. Transparent, self-serve pricingUxia and Spur both hide pricing behind a demo. A founder with a leaking funnel at 11pm wants to run a test now. We let them. That is a real distribution advantage against enterprise-motion competitors.
7. Reports improve retroactivelyOur evaluation runs on the saved recording, not live. When we improve our analysis, we can re-run it across every past session — customers' old reports get better for free, and we can prove our accuracy is improving.
8. We optimise for non-technical personasMost tools implicitly test a competent user. The money leaks where the confused first-timer gives up. That is deliberately where we aim.
9. Friction diff per release"Your onboarding got 2 steps harder this week." Nobody in category A or B can offer that, because human panels can't run weekly. This turns a one-off study into a subscription.
What we will not beat them at — and should not pretend to
  • Real human emotion. UserTesting gets you a person sighing in frustration on video. We never will. When emotional truth is the requirement, they win.
  • Brand, pricing and desirability research. That is category B's ground, not ours.
  • Deep bug detection. Momentic and QA Wolf will find more defects than we do. We are complementary to them, not a replacement.
  • Enterprise trust today. Security reviews and procurement will take us years to satisfy.
Being explicit about this is not weakness. A tool that claims to replace all user research will be dismissed by every serious researcher on first contact.

12Go-to-Market

The wedge: free public teardowns

Every week we run our own agent against a well-known product's sign-up flow and publish the report free: "We sent a non-technical first-time user through [Product]'s onboarding. Here is where they got stuck." This does four jobs at once — it is marketing content, it is proof the product works, it is our own accuracy testing, and it reliably reaches the product team at the company being reviewed. It is the cheapest distribution available to us, and it compounds.

13What We Are Building First (V0)

The only goal of V0One AI agent can open a real website in a real browser, work towards a goal on its own, record everything it did, and produce a report. Run from the command line. Nothing else.

In scope for V0

  • A command-line tool: run this goal, as this persona, on this website
  • The AI decision loop
  • Real browser control
  • Full recording of every step, with screenshots
  • The final structured report
  • Tests, run against our own small test website

Deliberately NOT in V0

  • No website / dashboard / UI
  • No customer accounts or logins
  • No billing
  • No cloud servers
  • No running many sessions at once
  • No automatic persona generation

Why so small? The only real risk in this business is whether the AI can genuinely use unfamiliar software like a person, and whether we can tell a real product problem from our own AI's confusion. Everything else — dashboards, accounts, billing — is ordinary work any competent team can do later. If the core does not work, none of the rest matters.

V0 is done when

  1. One command drives a real browser through a real website on its own.
  2. Every action the AI takes is checked, executed and recorded.
  3. Every run finishes with a clear outcome: SUCCESS FAILURE STUCK TIMEOUT ERROR — it can never hang forever.
  4. Every run produces a saved session and a structured report.
  5. The test suite passes.

14How It Works Under the Hood

1 · LOOKRead what is on the screen right now
↓
2 · UNDERSTANDWhere am I? What can I do here?
↓
3 · DECIDEWhat would this kind of person do next — and why?
↓
4 · ACTClick, type, scroll, or go back
↓
5 · SEE WHAT HAPPENEDDid it do what I expected?
↓
RECORD EVERYTHINGScreenshot · action · reasoning · result
↺ repeat until the goal is achieved, the AI gives up, or a limit is hit

The most important safety rule

The AI never writes or runs code. Ever.
It can only pick from a short, fixed menu of actions — click, type, select, scroll, press a key, go back, wait, finish, give up. And it can only point at things we have already shown it on the page; it cannot invent a target.

A separate piece of our software — not the AI — is the only thing that actually touches the browser. Every choice the AI makes is checked against strict rules before anything happens. This means the AI cannot do something unexpected even if it tried, and everything that happened is explainable afterwards.

16Safety Rules We Are Building In From Day One

  • The AI cannot spend money or destroy anything. Buttons like "Pay", "Purchase", "Delete" and "Send" are blocked at the software level — not merely discouraged in an instruction.
  • Passwords never reach the AI. The AI says "type the password here"; our software fills it in. The password never appears in the AI's input, its output, our logs, or our saved records.
  • Every session runs in a clean, isolated browser. Nothing carries over between runs.
  • The AI cannot wander off to other websites. It is locked to the site being tested.
  • Every run has a hard stop. Step limits, time limits, and detection of going in circles. Nothing can run forever or run up a bill.
  • We test on staging and demo environments, not on live systems holding real customer data.

  4. Do we agree on the one number? Percentage of findings a human agrees are genuine product problems. Above ~70% we continue, below ~40% we stop.
  5. How long do we give V0? Weeks, not months. Suggest a hard checkpoint at 6 weeks.
